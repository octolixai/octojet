512 experts, top-10), uniform random routing.

## Verdict under the stop rule: fail, kernel-limited

The rule (spec 6.1): the FP4 pipeline must be ≥ 1.5x faster than TensorFold at every prompt cell (R ≥ 512). It
reached 1.24x at R = 8,192. But the FP4 kernel runs at 8% of the FP4 peak, and the rule was meant to test the
format, not a first-draft kernel. The README's reading for this case: fail with the fp4 pipeline far below the fp4
peak while fp4 peak ≥ 3x bf16 → kernel-limited; the owner decides.

## Where the FP4 time goes

1. **Quantization is written serially.** Both quantize kernels give one thread a whole row (2,560 or 640 values,
   with a 7-step binary search per scale). At R = 1 that is 0.58 + 0.16 ms of the 0.85 ms total. At R = 8,192
   the two take 7.4 of 21.8 ms (34%). A warp per row, and SwiGLU + quantize fused into the up-GEMM epilogue (which
   also removes the 419 MB fp32 `h` round trip), should bring them near their memory floor (well under 1 ms).
2. **At decode sizes the GEMMs already beat TensorFold.** At R = 1 the two GEMMs take 0.108 ms and move ~27.6 MB
   of FP4 weights: about 94% of GB10's 273 GB/s. TensorFold's whole step takes 0.235 ms. Once quantization is
   fixed, FP4 would win at decode sizes too, where the spec says most of an agent step goes.
3. **At prompt sizes the GEMMs don't reuse weights.** Each 32-row item re-reads its expert's full weight slab from
   L2 or memory, with no shared-memory staging. The up GEMM reaches ~67 TFLOP/s at R = 8,192 (14% of peak), the
   down GEMM ~55. Larger row tiles (64-128) with weights staged in shared memory are the standard fix.

Rough ceiling if items 1-3 are fixed, taking GEMMs at 25% of the FP4 peak (TensorFold's efficiency against its own
bf16 peak): up ~4.6 ms, down ~2.3 ms, quantize ~0.5 ms, about 7 ms at R = 8,192 against TensorFold's 26.9 ms (~3.8x).
This is an estimate, not a measurement.

## Caveats

- TensorFold's decode plan is timed at R ≤ 128 and its prefill plan at R ≥ 512, as its engine chooses. At R = 16-128
  its decode plan (4.6-15.5 ms) is slower than its prefill plan at R = 512 (14.2 ms), so the FP4 ratios at 16-128 may
  flatter FP4. The prompt cells, which decide the verdict, are unaffected.
- Routing is uniform random. Real routing is skewed, which changes rows per expert. Both sides get the same
  distribution.
- W4A4: FP4 MMA quantizes activations to NVFP4. Accuracy is untested here (M8).
