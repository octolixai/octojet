## Correction to M1 (results/2026-09-28-m1.md)

M1 ran next to a live tf-serve. TensorFold measured about 2x slower there than here (R = 8192: 26.9 ms against
13.9 ms), while FP4 barely moved (21.8 against 22.4 ms). M1's "1.24-2x FP4 advantage" and the "TensorFold timings
are bimodal" note both came from a disturbed baseline, not from the kernels. Any future GPU comparison runs with
tf-serve paused, and both sides are timed twice.

## Why FP4 loses

- TensorFold's prefill expert kernel is well tuned: 57.9 TFLOP/s at R = 8192, about 49% of GB10's bf16 `mma.sync`
  peak (117.5 TFLOP/s). FP4's pipeline reaches 35.9 TFLOP/s, 7.6% of its 469.6 TFLOP/s peak.
- M1b's changes fixed the small-R quantize cost (R = 1: 0.58 → 0.021 ms) but not large R: at 8192 the fused up GEMM
  takes 12.5 ms (M1: up 8.0 + SwiGLU 4.2 ms), down 7.1 ms, quantize 2.8 ms.
- Clearing 1.5x at R = 8192 needs FP4 at ~87 TFLOP/s including quantization (~18% of peak). The hardware allows it,
  but it needs a production GEMM (multi-stage shared-memory pipelines, wider CTAs, A-tile reuse), weeks of work
  on top of a W4A4 accuracy risk.
- At decode sizes FP4 is at parity (R = 1: 0.144 vs 0.148 ms): its ~10% fewer weight bytes are too small a margin.

## What carries forward

- Conditional CUDA graphs work on sm_121a (5.89 µs per while-loop iteration): the GPU-resident decode loop
  (spec 5.3) is feasible.
- The block-scaled FP4 path is proven correct and row-invariant, and stays in `lab/m1` if FP4 is revisited
  (e.g. a CUTLASS-grade kernel, or a future checkpoint that needs it).
- Octojet's levers are now the prefix store, the GPU-resident decode loop and agent-aware drafting, on MLX 4-bit.
