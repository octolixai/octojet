## Verdict: F1 passes the stop rule on the context criterion; decode is workload-dependent

> **Correction (2026-09-29, after F1.1).** All F1 and F1.1 runs used TensorFold's default KV cache dtype (bf16,
> 30,784 B/token) on both engines. Production tf-serve on the Spark runs the MLX checkpoint with `--kv-dtype int8
> --parallel 2`, and its log says "2 streams of 262144 prompt/reply tokens (4799 MiB a stream) ... int8 KV cache (fp16
> scale per 32 values)". So with the production KV dtype, MLX 4-bit already reaches a 262,144 window per stream. The
> headline "262,144 vs 160,689 at the same footprint" below is therefore a bf16-KV artifact, not a gain over the
> production configuration. What NVFP4 does provide is about 7 GiB less resident weight (startup estimate 86.31 vs
> 87.49 GiB; cut-model allocation 8.732 vs 9.319 GiB, extrapolated to about 7.04 GiB, both under Memory). With int8 KV
> (18,304 B/token including the MTP head's cache, from TensorFold's recipe), 7 GiB is about 400k tokens of cache, i.e.
> potentially more concurrent 262k streams than MLX's 2 at `--parallel N`; this is unmeasured. Also measured in F1.1:
> with default KV and `--parallel 4` the windows were 135,367 (mixed) vs 74,856 (MLX) per stream.
>
> **Verdict, restated.** F1 meets the plan's written stop rule (window >= 200,000 at default settings), but that
> criterion did not reflect production, which runs int8 KV and already reaches 262,144 per stream on MLX weights. The
> measured NVFP4 benefit is about 7 GiB of resident memory; whether it buys additional concurrent 262k streams under
> int8 KV is the pending measurement (F1.2). Accuracy and kernel speed are at parity (F1.1: drafting-off decode
> identical; drafted decode differs only through MTP acceptance).
>
> **F1.2 (2026-09-29, measured; section "F1.2: int8 KV" below).** At the production KV dtype the 7 GiB buys
> concurrent long-context streams: `--parallel 3` gives the mixed checkpoint 3 x 262,144 where MLX gets 3 x 174,961,
> and `--parallel 4` gives 4 x 227,687 vs 4 x 120,136 (910,748 vs 480,544 cache tokens at the same ~87 GiB). The
> weight saving is confirmed at 7.03 GiB (startup estimates 80.16 vs 87.19 GiB with identical 2 x 262,144 caches).
> Concurrent outputs equal solo outputs at every N on both engines, and the mixed server serves 210k-260k prompts on
> one stream at 40-49 tok/s decode. **Final verdict: F1 passes; the gain is concurrent long-context capacity, not
> decode speed.**

Stop rule (spec section 6, F1 row): pass if decode is at least 5% above MLX (both passes agreeing within about 3%) OR
the window is at least 200,000, AND GSM8K is within paired noise (at most 3 net) and HumanEval pass@1 within 4
programs, AND drafted equals serial.

| Criterion | Result | Met |
|---|---|---|
| Window at least 200,000 | 262,144 (mixed, both passes) vs 160,689 / 160,204 (MLX, passes 1 / 2), same box | yes |
| Decode at least 5% above MLX in every cell | not demonstrated: workload-dependent (table below) | no |
| GSM8K within 3 net | 244/250 both; paired 2 only-mixed, 2 only-MLX (net 0) | yes |
| HumanEval within 4 programs | mixed 155/164, MLX 154/164 (net +1) | yes |
| Drafted equals serial | identical on all three prompts, output coherent (`f1-C2-exact.txt`, mixed pass 1) | yes |

So F1 passes the written context criterion (a bf16-KV result; see the correction above), with accuracy at parity and drafted equal to serial. Decode performance is
workload-dependent; the overall decode criterion (>= 5% faster in every cell) is not demonstrated: greedy chat +14.9% /
+15.3%, greedy code -25.2% / -25.1% (mixed vs MLX, passes 1 and 2); sampled cells within about ±6.2%. The 262,144 window is the
model's native maximum (`native 262144` in the startup line), so it is a cap, not a measured memory limit, and no
request of 200k tokens or more was exercised on the full model (the largest agent prompt is 153,641 tokens (MLX pass 1, turn 3; mixed reached 153,606)). The runs
carry no per-run commit metadata beyond the operator's reports.

### Decode speed is workload-dependent, with no overall gain

`bench_openai`, 64 tokens, median of 10 (`f1-C2-<engine>-<pass>-decode.json`), tok/s. Mixed vs MLX is the ratio of the
pass-1 medians and the pass-2 medians.

| Cell | Mixed pass 1 | Mixed pass 2 | MLX pass 1 | MLX pass 2 | Mixed / MLX (p1, p2) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Code (fibonacci-raw), sampled | 74.0 | 78.7 | 78.7 | 78.7 | 0.94, 1.00 |
| Chat (gpu-chat-no-think), sampled | 58.4 | 63.1 | 62.3 | 62.1 | 0.94, 1.01 |
| Code, greedy | 55.5 | 55.6 | 74.2 | 74.2 | 0.75, 0.75 |
| Chat, greedy | 83.2 | 83.2 | 72.4 | 72.2 | 1.15, 1.15 |

TTFT medians are 0.098-0.140 s for mixed and 0.101-0.146 s for MLX in the same cells (same files).

The two sampled cells are within about ±6.2% of MLX, but mixed's own pass-to-pass variation is 6.4% (sampled code) and 7.9%
(sampled chat) between its passes, above the roughly 3% repeatability criterion, so those cells do not show parity or
a difference.
The two greedy cells differ most and in opposite directions: mixed is about 25% slower on greedy code and about 15%
faster on greedy chat. Both passes agree in those cells. The caveat: these runs use MTP drafting (drafts: on), so
tok/s depends on how many drafts are accepted, and draft acceptance may differ between the two quantizations (different
logits, different greedy text; acceptance rates were not recorded). A greedy cell that runs 25% slower may be a lower acceptance rate rather than a slower
kernel. A kernel-speed comparison needs drafting off; that was not measured (follow-up below). Net: greedy chat is
consistently faster and greedy code consistently slower, so the decode branch of the stop rule is not met.

## What went wrong first, and how it was found

Run C attempt 1 (`results/f1-C-attempt1/`) served garbage from the mixed checkpoint (mixed scripts and code tokens in
every decode cell) although drafted equalled serial and the kernels were exact, so the model was deterministic and
wrong. The diagnostics (`results/f1-diag/`, tools `engine/tools/diag_expert_layer.py` and `diag_cut_compare.py`)
compared the mixed tables with MLX. Routed experts differed from MLX by the expected NVFP4 error (rel err 0.1243-0.1259
on the weights at layers 0 and 47; 0.16-0.17 on layer outputs, `act_rel_err_routed` 0.1619 and 0.1719), but the shared
expert (slot 512) was off by 8.6e6 (layer 0) and 1.4e7 (layer 47) in the gate/up activations (`act_rel_err_shared`; the
down-projection output error `y_rel_err_shared` was 2.41e10 and 4.22e10), with weight norm ratios of
about 2,300-3,500 against MLX (`norm_ratio` in the shared pairs). The cut model (4 layers, 96 tokens) agreed with
neither: logits cos 0.08, top-1 agreement 0.0. The local `-fp8hybrid` export stores the shared experts as
`float8_e4m3fn` with a 128x128 block `weight_scale_inv` (multiplied, despite the name) and the loader had read the
raw fp8 codes without the scale (`export_absmax` 448.0 vs the MLX 0.77). Fix bf48993 dequantizes with the scale
before NVFP4 quantization, raises on fp8 without a scale, and guards absmax above 16. After the fix
(`results/f1-diag2/`): shared-expert weight pairs cos 0.9912-0.9918, rel err 0.1269-0.1318; gate/up activation error for the
shared slot (`act_rel_err_shared`) 0.1635 (layer 0) and 0.1429 (layer 47), and down-projection output error
(`y_rel_err_shared`) 0.199 and 0.196, comparable to routed; the check tool passes 48/48 pairs including
12 shared-expert pairs (`f1-B3`, layers 0, 3, 24, 47, `shared_source_dtype` float8_e4m3fn); the cut-model logits have
cos 0.99055 (min 0.894) and top-1 agreement 0.75. That post-fix cut comparison still records `ok: false` and
`verdict: mixed_cut_wrong`, because top-1 agreement 0.75 is below the tool's 0.8 bar (cos 0.9906, rel_err 0.128; the median
rank of MLX's top-1 token in the mixed logits is 0; the artifact records only the median, not per-position ranks). My reading, an interpretation and not a measurement,
is that a random-token 4-layer cut has flat logits, so top-1 is a weak measure. The `prefill_vs_onerow` comparison was
fixed in 24bef0d but the post-fix cut has `prefill_rows: 0`, so it was not re-run on real data. C2 and D were then run on 24bef0d.

## Memory

The mixed startup estimate is 86.31 GiB vs 87.49 GiB (MLX pass 1), 1.18 GiB lower. The estimate is a wrapper
(`estimate_transform` in `engine/src/tensorfold/families/qwen4_exp/cuda/nvfp4.py`): it reads the MLX checkpoint headers
and rescales the routed-expert tensors of the decoder layers by 144/160 (NVFP4 words per block against affine); the
MTP head's experts stay affine, and nothing else is changed. The measured CUDA allocation on the 4-layer cut model
(`f1-diag-cut-cmp.json`, capacity 160,768) was 8.732 GiB mixed vs 9.319 GiB MLX, 0.587 GiB lower for four layers (the
producer divides by 2**30; the field is named `cuda_allocated_gb`). If that
scaled linearly to 48 layers it would be approximately 7.04 GiB, larger than the 1.18 GiB in the startup estimate. The two
numbers were not reconciled here (the 7.04 GiB is my extrapolation, not a measurement; part of the expert tables may live
host-mapped so that the estimate counts less), and no full-model CUDA allocation was recorded for either engine. What
the files show for certain: the mixed estimate is lower, and the mixed server reached the model's full native
262,144 window while MLX reached 160,689 / 160,204 on the same box.
